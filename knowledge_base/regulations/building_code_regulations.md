# Unified Building Code Regulations (UBCR)

**Issued by the National Building Authority**
**Edition 2026 — Consolidated Text**

---

## Chapter 1 — General Provisions, Scope and Administration

### Article 1.1 — Title and Citation

This regulatory instrument shall be cited as the Unified Building Code Regulations, hereinafter referred to as "this Code." This Code is issued by the National Building Authority, hereinafter referred to as "the Authority," and establishes the minimum mandatory technical requirements for the design, construction, alteration, extension, repair, occupancy, maintenance and demolition of buildings and building services within the territorial jurisdiction of the Authority. Where any provision of this Code conflicts with a provision of a superseded edition, the provision of this Code shall prevail from its effective date.

### Article 1.2 — Purpose and Objectives

The purpose of this Code is to safeguard public health, safety and general welfare by establishing minimum performance and prescriptive requirements for structural stability, fire resistance, means of egress, sanitation, adequate natural light and ventilation, energy efficiency, and accessibility for persons with disabilities. The requirements of this Code represent minimum acceptable standards. Nothing in this Code shall be construed to prohibit a designer, owner or contractor from adopting higher standards of safety or performance, provided that all minimum requirements stated in this Code are satisfied in full.

### Article 1.3 — Scope of Application

This Code applies to all new buildings, to all additions to existing buildings, to changes of occupancy classification, and to alterations affecting structural elements, fire-resistance ratings, means of egress, or building services. This Code does not apply to temporary structures erected for a period not exceeding one hundred eighty (180) consecutive days, to agricultural storage structures with no human occupancy and a gross floor area not exceeding 100 m², or to works of civil infrastructure such as bridges, dams, roads and tunnels, which are governed by separate technical instruments issued by the Authority.

### Article 1.4 — Existing Buildings and Non-Conforming Conditions

An existing building lawfully constructed under a previous edition of the building regulations may continue in its existing use and configuration without full compliance with this Code, provided that no condition constituting an immediate hazard to life safety is present. Where an alteration affects more than forty percent (40%) of the gross floor area of an existing building within any period of thirty-six (36) months, the entire affected storey shall be brought into compliance with the fire safety, means of egress and accessibility provisions of this Code. Repair of damage caused by fire, flood or seismic action shall restore the building to a condition not less safe than its condition prior to the damage.

### Article 1.5 — Permits and Prior Approval

No person shall commence construction, alteration, extension or demolition of any building subject to this Code without first obtaining a written building permit from the Authority. An application for a building permit shall be accompanied by architectural drawings, structural calculations and drawings, mechanical, electrical and plumbing drawings, a soil investigation report where required by Article 5.2 of this Code, and a statement of compliance signed by a licensed engineer. The Authority shall issue a decision on a complete application within thirty (30) working days of receipt. A building permit shall lapse if substantial construction has not commenced within twelve (12) months of issuance.

### Article 1.6 — Inspections During Construction

The permit holder shall notify the Authority and obtain inspection approval at the following mandatory stages: completion of excavation and prior to placement of foundation reinforcement; completion of foundation concrete; completion of the structural frame of each storey; completion of rough-in of electrical, plumbing and mechanical systems prior to concealment; installation of fire protection systems prior to concealment; and completion of all works prior to occupancy. Concealment of any work that has not been inspected and approved shall be a violation of this Code, and the Authority may require the exposure of such work at the expense of the permit holder.

### Article 1.7 — Certificate of Occupancy

No building or portion of a building shall be occupied until the Authority has issued a Certificate of Occupancy. The Certificate of Occupancy shall state the permitted occupancy classification, the maximum permitted occupant load of each storey, and the edition of this Code under which the building was approved. Occupation of a building without a valid Certificate of Occupancy shall subject the owner to administrative penalties and to an order of evacuation. A Certificate of Occupancy issued for one occupancy classification shall not authorize occupancy under a different classification.

### Article 1.8 — Alternative Materials, Designs and Methods

The Authority may approve an alternative material, design or construction method not expressly prescribed by this Code where the applicant demonstrates, by testing conducted at an accredited laboratory, by recognized engineering analysis, or by documented performance in comparable installations, that the proposed alternative is equivalent or superior to the prescribed requirement in respect of structural strength, fire resistance, durability, safety and sanitation. The burden of demonstrating equivalence rests entirely with the applicant. An approval granted under this Article applies only to the specific project for which it was requested and shall not constitute a general precedent.

### Article 1.9 — Penalties and Enforcement

A person who constructs, alters or occupies a building in violation of this Code commits an administrative offence. The Authority may issue a stop-work order, impose a financial penalty proportionate to the gravity of the violation and the value of the works, order the correction of the violation within a stated period, or order the demolition of unlawful works. Where a violation creates an imminent danger to life, the Authority may order immediate evacuation of the building without prior notice. Financial penalties shall not relieve the violator of the obligation to bring the works into compliance.

## Chapter 2 — Definitions and Units of Measurement

### Article 2.1 — Units of Measurement

All dimensions, areas, volumes, loads and capacities stated in this Code are expressed in the International System of Units (SI). Linear dimensions are expressed in metres (m) or millimetres (mm); areas in square metres (m²); volumes in cubic metres (m³); forces in kilonewtons (kN); pressures and stresses in kilopascals (kPa) or megapascals (MPa); illuminance in lux (lx); and sound levels in A-weighted decibels (dB(A)). Where a dimension is stated as a minimum, the constructed dimension shall be equal to or greater than the stated value; where stated as a maximum, the constructed dimension shall be equal to or less than the stated value.

### Article 2.2 — Definition of Habitable Room

For the purposes of this Code, a habitable room means an enclosed space within a dwelling unit or residential occupancy intended for living, sleeping, eating or food preparation on a continuous or regular basis. Living rooms, bedrooms, dining rooms, family rooms and kitchens are habitable rooms. Bathrooms, water closets, laundry rooms, storage rooms, corridors, staircases, utility rooms, garages and spaces with a clear ceiling height below 2.10 m are not habitable rooms and are not subject to the minimum area, daylight and ventilation requirements applicable to habitable rooms.

### Article 2.3 — Definition of Floor Area

Net floor area means the area of a room measured between the inner faces of the enclosing walls, excluding the area occupied by built-in structural columns, chimneys, ducts and fixed partitions. Gross floor area means the total area of all storeys of a building measured to the outer face of the external walls, including internal walls, shafts and circulation spaces. Unless expressly stated otherwise, all room area requirements in this Code refer to net floor area, and all building area requirements refer to gross floor area.

### Article 2.4 — Definition of Storey, Basement and Mezzanine

A storey is the portion of a building between the upper surface of a floor and the upper surface of the floor immediately above. A basement is a storey in which the finished floor level is more than 1.20 m below the average adjoining finished ground level. A mezzanine is an intermediate floor level within a storey having a net floor area not exceeding one-third (1/3) of the net floor area of the room or space in which it is located; a mezzanine exceeding this proportion shall be counted as a separate storey for all purposes of this Code, including building height limits and means of egress calculations.

### Article 2.5 — Definition of Occupant Load

Occupant load means the number of persons for which the means of egress of a building or portion of a building is designed. The occupant load shall be calculated by dividing the net floor area of each space by the occupant load factor applicable to its use, as follows: residential, 18.5 m² per person; business and office, 9.0 m² per person; educational classrooms, 2.0 m² per person; assembly with fixed seating, one person per seat; assembly without fixed seating, 0.70 m² per person; mercantile ground floor, 3.0 m² per person; and industrial, 9.0 m² per person. Where a space is subject to multiple uses, the use producing the highest occupant load shall govern.

### Article 2.6 — Occupancy Classification

Every building or portion of a building shall be assigned one or more of the following occupancy classifications: Group A (Assembly), comprising spaces for gathering of persons for worship, recreation, dining or entertainment; Group B (Business), comprising offices, professional services and laboratories; Group E (Educational), comprising schools and instructional facilities; Group H (Hazardous), comprising facilities storing or processing materials presenting a physical or health hazard; Group I (Institutional), comprising hospitals, care facilities and detention facilities; Group M (Mercantile), comprising retail sales facilities; Group R (Residential), comprising dwelling units, apartments, hotels and dormitories; and Group S (Storage). Where a building contains more than one classification, the most restrictive requirement applicable to any classification present shall govern shared elements.

## Chapter 3 — Room Dimensions and Habitability

### Article 3.1 — Minimum Floor Area of Habitable Rooms

Every habitable room in a residential occupancy shall have a minimum net floor area of twelve square metres (12 m²). This minimum shall be measured in accordance with the definition of net floor area given in Article 2.3 of this Code, excluding areas beneath any portion of the ceiling with a clear height of less than 2.10 m. Where a dwelling unit contains more than one habitable room, at least one room shall have a minimum net floor area of fourteen square metres (14 m²) and shall serve as the principal living space. Kitchens intended solely for food preparation and not for dining are exempt from the 12 m² minimum and are governed by Article 3.4 of this Code.

### Article 3.2 — Minimum Room Width and Dimensional Proportion

No habitable room shall have any horizontal dimension less than 2.40 m measured between the inner faces of the enclosing walls. A habitable room shall not be configured such that its length exceeds three (3) times its width, unless the portion of the room exceeding this proportion is disregarded in the calculation of the minimum net floor area required by Article 3.1 of this Code. Alcoves, recesses and bay projections having a depth not exceeding 1.20 m may be included in the calculation of net floor area, provided that their width is not less than 1.80 m.

### Article 3.3 — Minimum Clear Ceiling Height

The minimum clear ceiling height, measured vertically from the finished floor surface to the lowest point of the finished ceiling surface, shall be 2.60 m in habitable rooms of residential occupancies, 2.70 m in offices, classrooms and retail spaces, and 3.00 m in assembly spaces with an occupant load exceeding fifty (50) persons. In bathrooms, water closets, kitchens, corridors, storage rooms and garages, the minimum clear ceiling height shall be 2.20 m. Beams, ducts, sprinkler piping and light fixtures may project below the required ceiling height to a minimum clearance of 2.10 m, provided that such projections do not occupy more than fifteen percent (15%) of the ceiling area of the space.

### Article 3.4 — Kitchens and Food Preparation Spaces

A kitchen in a dwelling unit shall have a minimum net floor area of 5.0 m² and a minimum clear width of 1.80 m where fixtures are arranged along one wall only, or 2.40 m where fixtures are arranged along two opposing walls. A minimum clear working passage of 1.00 m shall be maintained between opposing counters, appliances and fixtures. Every kitchen shall be provided with a continuous impervious work surface of not less than 1.80 linear metres, a sink connected to hot and cold potable water, and mechanical exhaust ventilation complying with Article 7.5 of this Code. Wall surfaces behind cooking appliances and sinks shall be finished with a non-absorbent, washable material to a height of not less than 0.60 m above the work surface.

### Article 3.5 — Bathrooms and Water Closet Compartments

Every dwelling unit shall contain at least one bathroom equipped with a water closet, a washbasin and a bathtub or shower. A room containing a water closet shall have a minimum net floor area of 1.50 m² and a minimum clear width of 0.90 m. A clear space of not less than 0.60 m in width shall be provided in front of every water closet, and a lateral clearance of not less than 0.20 m shall be provided between the centreline of the water closet and any adjacent wall or fixture. Floors of bathrooms shall be finished with impervious, slip-resistant materials, and wall surfaces adjacent to bathtubs and showers shall be impervious to a height of not less than 1.80 m above the finished floor.

### Article 3.6 — Sleeping Rooms and Emergency Escape Openings

Every sleeping room located in a basement or on any storey of a residential occupancy shall be provided with at least one emergency escape and rescue opening giving direct access to the exterior or to a court or yard open to a public way. Such opening shall have a minimum clear openable area of 0.50 m², a minimum clear openable width of 0.50 m, and a minimum clear openable height of 0.60 m. The bottom of the clear opening shall be located not more than 1.10 m above the finished floor of the room. The opening shall be operable from the inside without the use of keys, tools or special knowledge.

### Article 3.7 — Access and Internal Circulation Within Dwelling Units

Every habitable room shall be accessible without passing through a bathroom, water closet compartment or utility room. A bedroom shall not serve as the sole means of access to another bedroom, except where the second room is a dressing room, a study or a nursery directly associated with the first. Internal corridors within a dwelling unit shall have a minimum clear width of 0.90 m. Door openings within a dwelling unit shall provide a clear opening width of not less than 0.75 m, except for bathroom doors, which shall provide a clear opening width of not less than 0.70 m.

### Article 3.8 — Prohibition of Habitation in Unsuitable Spaces

No space shall be used or approved as a habitable room where it is located wholly below the adjoining finished ground level on all sides, where it lacks the natural daylight required by Article 4.1 of this Code, where its clear ceiling height is less than the minimum prescribed by Article 3.3 of this Code, or where it contains fuel-fired equipment not separated from the habitable space by a fire-rated enclosure. Spaces used for the storage of flammable liquids, gas cylinders or hazardous chemicals shall not open directly into a habitable room.

### Article 3.9 — Storage, Utility and Service Rooms Within Dwellings

Every dwelling unit shall be provided with enclosed storage space of not less than 2.0 m³ of volume, which may be distributed among one or more locations within the unit. A utility or laundry room containing a washing machine shall be provided with a floor drain, a water supply connection with an isolating valve, and either an openable window or mechanical exhaust ventilation providing not less than 15 litres per second of extract capacity. Water heaters and boilers installed within a dwelling unit shall be located in a ventilated enclosure and shall not be installed within a sleeping room or a bathroom.

## Chapter 4 — Natural Lighting and Daylight

### Article 4.1 — Minimum Window Area for Natural Lighting

Every habitable room shall be provided with natural daylight by means of one or more windows or glazed openings located in an external wall. The aggregate clear glazed area of such openings shall be not less than ten percent (10%) of the net floor area of the room served. For the purposes of this calculation, the clear glazed area excludes window frames, mullions, transoms and any permanently obstructed portion of the opening. Where a habitable room is served by a skylight or roof light, the glazed area of the skylight may be credited at one hundred fifty percent (150%) of its actual area, provided that the total credited area does not exceed fifty percent (50%) of the required glazed area for that room.

### Article 4.2 — Height of Window Sill Above Finished Floor

The sill of every window serving a habitable room shall be located at a height of not less than 0.80 m and not more than 1.10 m above the finished floor level of the room served, measured vertically from the finished floor to the upper surface of the interior window sill. Where a window sill is located at a height of less than 0.80 m above the finished floor and the external drop below the window exceeds 0.60 m, a protective guard or fixed barrier complying with Article 4.3 of this Code shall be installed. Window sills in bathrooms, kitchens and non-habitable service spaces are exempt from the maximum sill height limit of 1.10 m.

### Article 4.3 — Guards at Low-Level Glazing and Openings

Where the finished floor level on the interior side of a window, glazed door or opening is more than 0.60 m above the adjacent external surface, and the lower edge of the glazed opening is less than 0.80 m above the interior finished floor, a guard shall be provided. The guard shall have a minimum height of 1.10 m above the interior finished floor, shall be constructed to resist a horizontal line load of not less than 0.75 kN/m applied at its upper edge, and shall be configured so that a sphere of 100 mm diameter cannot pass through any opening in the guard. Guards shall not incorporate horizontal elements or ornamental patterns that facilitate climbing by children.

### Article 4.4 — Obstruction of Daylight by Adjacent Construction

A window shall not be credited toward the natural daylight requirement of Article 4.1 of this Code where it faces an obstruction located at a horizontal distance less than one-half (1/2) of the height of that obstruction measured above the window sill level, unless the horizontal distance is not less than 3.00 m. A window opening onto an internal light well shall be credited only where the light well has a minimum horizontal dimension of 3.00 m and a minimum plan area of 12 m², and is open to the sky over its entire area. Light wells serving buildings exceeding four (4) storeys shall have their minimum horizontal dimension increased by 0.50 m for each additional storey.

### Article 4.5 — Artificial Illumination Levels

Where natural daylight is insufficient or unavailable, artificial illumination shall be provided at the following minimum maintained illuminance levels measured at the working plane 0.80 m above the finished floor: 100 lx in corridors, lobbies and storage rooms; 150 lx in residential living rooms and bedrooms; 200 lx in kitchens and bathrooms; 300 lx in offices and retail sales areas; 500 lx in classrooms, drawing offices and detailed assembly work areas; and 50 lx in car parks and plant rooms. Illumination of stairways and landings shall be not less than 100 lx measured at the tread surface, and shall be controlled so that no stairway is left unilluminated during occupancy.

### Article 4.6 — Glazing Materials and Safety Glass

Safety glazing complying with the impact resistance requirements of a recognized international standard shall be installed in all of the following locations: glazed doors and side panels within 0.30 m of a door edge and less than 1.50 m above the finished floor; glazing in bathtub and shower enclosures where the lower edge is less than 1.50 m above the standing surface; glazed panels having an individual pane area exceeding 1.00 m² with a lower edge less than 0.50 m above the finished floor; and all glazed guards and balustrades. Safety glazing shall be permanently marked by the manufacturer with an identification of the standard to which it conforms.

## Chapter 5 — Structural Requirements

### Article 5.1 — General Structural Performance Objectives

Every building shall be designed and constructed to safely resist all loads and load combinations to which it may reasonably be subjected during its intended service life without loss of stability, without exceeding permissible deflections, and without local or progressive collapse. The structural system shall provide a continuous load path from the point of load application to the foundation and thence to the supporting soil. Structural design shall be carried out by a licensed structural engineer, and the design documents shall state the design working life of the structure, which shall be not less than fifty (50) years for permanent buildings.

### Article 5.2 — Geotechnical Investigation Requirements

A geotechnical investigation report prepared by a qualified geotechnical engineer shall be submitted with the permit application for any building exceeding two (2) storeys in height, any building with a gross floor area exceeding 500 m², any building incorporating a basement, and any building located on a site with known expansive soils, fill material, high groundwater or slope instability. The investigation shall include a minimum of one borehole per 400 m² of building footprint, with a minimum of three boreholes per site, extending to a depth of not less than 1.5 times the least plan dimension of the foundation or to refusal on competent bedrock. The report shall state the recommended foundation type, the allowable bearing capacity, the expected settlement, the groundwater level and the site seismic classification.

### Article 5.3 — Minimum Design Live Loads

Structural design shall assume the following minimum uniformly distributed live loads acting on floor areas: residential rooms, 2.0 kPa; residential balconies, 3.0 kPa; offices, 2.5 kPa; corridors and lobbies serving assembly occupancies, 5.0 kPa; classrooms, 3.0 kPa; retail sales areas, 5.0 kPa; light storage, 6.0 kPa; assembly areas with movable seating, 5.0 kPa; stairs and escape routes, 4.0 kPa; and accessible flat roofs used for maintenance only, 1.0 kPa. These values shall not be reduced except in accordance with the live load reduction provisions applicable to tributary areas exceeding 35 m², and in no case shall the reduction exceed forty percent (40%) of the tabulated value.

### Article 5.4 — Wind Load Design

Buildings shall be designed to resist wind pressures derived from a basic wind speed of not less than 35 m/s measured as a three-second gust at 10 m above ground in open terrain, adjusted for site exposure category, topographic effects and building height. The structural frame, the cladding and the roof covering shall each be designed for the wind pressures applicable to their respective tributary areas, with increased local pressure coefficients applied at roof edges, eaves, ridges and building corners. Roof coverings, parapets, canopies and externally mounted equipment shall be positively anchored to resist wind uplift, and reliance on gravity alone for the restraint of roof elements is prohibited.

### Article 5.5 — Seismic Design Requirements

Buildings shall be designed to resist seismic forces determined from the site seismic classification stated in the geotechnical report required by Article 5.2 of this Code. The lateral force resisting system shall be clearly identified in the design documents and shall be continuous from the roof to the foundation. Buildings exceeding four (4) storeys shall be provided with a lateral force resisting system consisting of shear walls, braced frames or moment-resisting frames detailed for ductile behaviour. Structural irregularities including soft storeys, discontinuous shear walls, re-entrant plan corners exceeding fifteen percent (15%) of the plan dimension, and mass irregularities exceeding fifty percent (50%) between adjacent storeys shall be identified in the design documents and shall be subjected to dynamic analysis.

### Article 5.6 — Concrete Construction Requirements

Structural concrete shall have a minimum characteristic compressive cylinder strength at twenty-eight (28) days of 25 MPa for reinforced structural elements, 20 MPa for plain concrete foundations and blinding above 10 MPa, and 30 MPa for elements permanently exposed to soil, groundwater or marine environments. Concrete shall be sampled and tested at a frequency of not less than one set of three specimens per 50 m³ placed or per storey of each structural element type, whichever produces more frequent testing. Cold joints, honeycombing and segregation shall be repaired under the direction of the structural engineer before any subsequent element is placed upon the affected member.

### Article 5.7 — Reinforcement Cover and Detailing

The minimum clear concrete cover to reinforcing steel shall be 20 mm for slabs and walls not exposed to weather, 25 mm for beams and columns not exposed to weather, 40 mm for elements exposed to weather or in contact with backfill, and 75 mm for concrete cast directly against soil without formwork. Lap splices in reinforcement shall not be located at points of maximum stress and shall not exceed fifty percent (50%) of the bars at any one section unless specifically analysed. Reinforcement shall be free of loose rust, oil, paint and other substances that impair bond, and shall be securely supported to maintain the specified cover during concrete placement.

### Article 5.8 — Masonry and Load-Bearing Walls

Load-bearing masonry walls shall have a minimum nominal thickness of 200 mm for single-storey construction and 250 mm for the lowest storey of two-storey construction. The unsupported height of a load-bearing masonry wall shall not exceed twenty (20) times its nominal thickness. Masonry units shall have a minimum compressive strength of 7.0 MPa for load-bearing applications and 3.5 MPa for non-load-bearing partitions. Horizontal reinforcement shall be provided in bed joints at vertical intervals not exceeding 0.60 m, and reinforced concrete bond beams shall be provided at each floor level and at the head of all openings exceeding 1.20 m in width.

### Article 5.9 — Foundations and Bearing

Foundations shall be founded below the zone of seasonal moisture variation and at a depth of not less than 0.60 m below the lowest adjacent finished ground level. Footings shall not be founded on topsoil, uncontrolled fill, organic material or frozen ground. Where adjacent footings are founded at different levels, the line connecting the nearest edges of the two footings shall not make an angle with the horizontal exceeding thirty degrees (30°) in soil or sixty degrees (60°) in rock. Differential settlement between adjacent columns shall be limited to one three-hundredth (1/300) of the span between them.

### Article 5.10 — Balconies, Cantilevers and Projections

Balconies and cantilevered slabs shall be designed for the live load specified in Article 5.3 of this Code and shall be provided with positive drainage away from the building envelope and with a continuous waterproofing membrane turned up not less than 0.15 m at all abutting walls. The finished level of a balcony shall be not less than 0.05 m below the adjacent internal finished floor level. Every balcony, terrace, roof and elevated walkway with a drop exceeding 0.60 m shall be provided with a guard not less than 1.10 m in height complying with the loading and opening limitations of Article 4.3 of this Code.

### Article 5.11 — Structural Inspection and Testing During Construction

The structural engineer of record or a designated representative shall perform and document inspection of the following: foundation bearing surfaces prior to placement of concrete; reinforcement placement, spacing and cover prior to concrete placement; formwork geometry and shoring prior to concrete placement; welding of structural steel connections; installation of post-installed anchors; and post-tensioning operations. Inspection records shall be retained by the owner for the design working life of the structure and shall be produced to the Authority upon request.

## Chapter 6 — Fire Safety and Fire Protection

### Article 6.1 — Fire-Resistance Rating of Structural Elements

Structural elements shall be provided with fire-resistance ratings, expressed in minutes of resistance to a standard time-temperature exposure, as follows: buildings not exceeding two (2) storeys, 60 minutes for load-bearing elements; buildings of three (3) to five (5) storeys, 90 minutes; buildings of six (6) to eleven (11) storeys, 120 minutes; and buildings of twelve (12) storeys or more, 180 minutes. The fire-resistance rating of a structural element shall be achieved by the inherent properties of the material, by applied protective coatings or boards tested as a system, or by a combination thereof, and the tested assembly shall be documented in the permit submission.

### Article 6.2 — Compartmentation and Fire Barriers

Every building shall be divided into fire compartments so that the gross floor area of any single compartment does not exceed 1,500 m² in residential and business occupancies, 1,000 m² in institutional and assembly occupancies, and 2,500 m² in single-storey storage and industrial occupancies where an automatic sprinkler system is installed throughout. Fire barriers forming compartment boundaries shall have a fire-resistance rating of not less than 120 minutes, shall be continuous from the floor slab to the underside of the floor or roof slab above, and shall be sealed at all penetrations with tested fire-stopping systems of a rating equal to that of the barrier penetrated.

### Article 6.3 — Separation Between Dwelling Units

Walls and floors separating one dwelling unit from another dwelling unit, and separating a dwelling unit from a common corridor, stairway, plant room or car park, shall have a fire-resistance rating of not less than 60 minutes. Such separations shall be continuous and shall not be penetrated by unprotected openings. Service ducts, pipework and cable penetrations crossing these separations shall be fire-stopped to maintain the required rating. Doors opening from a dwelling unit onto a common corridor shall be self-closing fire doors with a fire-resistance rating of not less than 30 minutes fitted with intumescent and smoke seals.

### Article 6.4 — Protection of Vertical Shafts and Stairways

Stairways, lift shafts, refuse chutes, and service and ventilation shafts connecting two or more storeys shall be enclosed in construction having a fire-resistance rating of not less than 120 minutes where they connect four (4) or more storeys, and not less than 60 minutes where they connect fewer than four storeys. Openings into such enclosures shall be protected by self-closing fire doors having a fire-resistance rating of not less than one-half of the rating of the enclosure, with a minimum of 30 minutes. Lift lobbies serving buildings exceeding six (6) storeys shall be constructed as protected lobbies with a minimum 60-minute fire-resistance rating.

### Article 6.5 — Surface Finish and Interior Lining Materials

Interior surface finishes shall be classified for surface spread of flame and for smoke production. Materials with the highest ratings for limited flame spread and limited smoke production shall be used in protected stairways, escape corridors, and lift lobbies. Materials of intermediate classification are permitted in rooms with a floor area not exceeding 30 m². The use of combustible insulation, polymeric foam or unprotected timber lining within a protected escape route is prohibited. Textile wall coverings and suspended decorative materials within assembly occupancies shall be treated with a durable flame-retardant treatment and shall be retreated at intervals recommended by the manufacturer.

### Article 6.6 — External Wall Cladding and Façade Fire Performance

External wall assemblies on buildings exceeding four (4) storeys or 15.0 m in height shall be constructed of materials of limited combustibility, or shall be demonstrated by a full-scale façade fire test to resist vertical fire spread. Cavity barriers shall be provided within ventilated façade cavities at every storey level, at the perimeter of every opening, and at horizontal intervals not exceeding 10.0 m. The vertical distance between the head of an opening and the sill of the opening directly above on the same façade shall be not less than 0.90 m of imperforate fire-resisting construction, unless an approved alternative such as a projecting horizontal fire barrier of not less than 0.60 m is provided.

### Article 6.7 — Automatic Sprinkler Systems

An automatic sprinkler system shall be installed throughout the following buildings: all buildings exceeding six (6) storeys or 22.0 m in height; all assembly occupancies with an occupant load exceeding three hundred (300) persons; all institutional occupancies; all mercantile occupancies with a gross floor area exceeding 2,000 m²; all enclosed car parks exceeding 1,000 m²; and all storage occupancies with a storage height exceeding 4.0 m. Sprinkler systems shall be supplied from a dedicated water source capable of sustaining the design density over the design area for a minimum duration of 60 minutes, and shall be fitted with a monitored flow switch reporting to the building fire alarm control panel.

### Article 6.8 — Fire Detection and Alarm Systems

An automatic fire detection and alarm system shall be installed in all buildings exceeding three (3) storeys, all assembly occupancies with an occupant load exceeding one hundred (100) persons, all institutional and educational occupancies, and all buildings containing sleeping accommodation other than single-family dwellings. Smoke detectors shall be installed in corridors, lobbies, stairway enclosures, plant rooms and electrical rooms. Every dwelling unit shall be provided with interconnected smoke alarms located in each sleeping room, immediately outside each sleeping area, and on every storey. Audible alarm signals shall achieve a sound pressure level of not less than 65 dB(A) throughout the occupied space, or 75 dB(A) at the pillow position within sleeping rooms.

### Article 6.9 — Portable Fire Extinguishers and Hose Reels

Portable fire extinguishers shall be distributed so that the maximum travel distance from any point in the building to the nearest extinguisher does not exceed 20.0 m for ordinary combustible hazards and 15.0 m for flammable liquid hazards. Extinguishers shall be mounted with the carrying handle between 0.90 m and 1.40 m above the finished floor, shall be clearly signed, and shall be inspected at intervals not exceeding twelve (12) months. Fire hose reels shall be provided in buildings exceeding 1,000 m² of gross floor area per storey, located adjacent to exits and protected stairway entrances, and shall be capable of delivering not less than 0.5 litres per second at the nozzle with a coverage radius of 30.0 m.

### Article 6.10 — Fire Department Access and Fire-Fighting Shafts

Every building shall be provided with vehicular access for fire-fighting appliances along not less than one-sixth (1/6) of the building perimeter, with an access roadway having a minimum clear width of 4.0 m, a minimum overhead clearance of 4.5 m, and a design load capacity of not less than 15 tonnes per axle. Buildings exceeding six (6) storeys or 22.0 m in height shall be provided with at least one fire-fighting shaft containing a fire-fighting lift, a protected stairway and a fire main outlet at each storey. The fire-fighting lift shall be provided with a secondary power supply complying with Article 9.7 of this Code and shall be capable of reaching the topmost storey within sixty (60) seconds of activation.

### Article 6.11 — Smoke Control and Ventilation of Escape Routes

Protected stairways serving buildings exceeding four (4) storeys shall be provided with smoke control by natural ventilation through an openable vent of not less than 1.0 m² of free area at the head of the stairway, or by a mechanical pressurization system maintaining a differential pressure of between 15 Pa and 60 Pa relative to the adjoining accommodation with all doors closed. Basements exceeding 200 m² in area shall be provided with smoke venting of not less than 2.5 percent of the floor area, or by a mechanical extract system providing not less than ten (10) air changes per hour and constructed of ductwork rated for operation at 300 °C for not less than 60 minutes.

### Article 6.12 — Storage and Handling of Flammable Materials

Rooms used for the storage of flammable liquids, liquefied petroleum gas cylinders or aerosols shall be separated from all other parts of the building by construction having a fire-resistance rating of not less than 120 minutes, shall be provided with permanent low-level and high-level ventilation openings each of not less than 0.10 m² of free area, shall be fitted with explosion-proof electrical equipment, and shall not be located below the lowest storey of the building or adjacent to a protected escape route. Storage of liquefied petroleum gas cylinders within a basement, a stairway enclosure or a corridor is prohibited under all circumstances.

## Chapter 7 — Means of Egress and Emergency Evacuation

### Article 7.1 — Number of Exits Required

Every storey of a building shall be provided with not less than two (2) independent exits, except that a single exit is permitted from a storey where the occupant load does not exceed thirty (30) persons, the travel distance to the exit does not exceed 15.0 m, and the storey is located not more than one storey above or below the level of exit discharge. A storey with an occupant load exceeding five hundred (500) persons shall be provided with not less than three (3) exits, and a storey with an occupant load exceeding one thousand (1,000) persons shall be provided with not less than four (4) exits.

### Article 7.2 — Separation and Remoteness of Exits

Where two exits are required, they shall be separated by a distance not less than one-half (1/2) of the maximum diagonal dimension of the area served, measured in a straight line between the nearest edges of the exit doorways. Where the building is protected throughout by an automatic sprinkler system complying with Article 6.7 of this Code, this separation may be reduced to one-third (1/3) of the maximum diagonal dimension. Where three or more exits are required, at least two of them shall satisfy the separation requirement of this Article, and the remaining exits shall be arranged to serve the occupant load reasonably.

### Article 7.3 — Maximum Travel Distance to an Exit

The travel distance measured along the natural path of travel from the most remote occupiable point on a storey to the nearest exit door shall not exceed the following limits: 30.0 m in residential occupancies; 35.0 m in business and mercantile occupancies; 25.0 m in institutional occupancies; 30.0 m in assembly occupancies; and 20.0 m in hazardous occupancies. Where the building is protected throughout by an automatic sprinkler system, these distances may be increased by twenty-five percent (25%). Travel distance within a dead-end corridor shall not exceed 7.5 m in any occupancy.

### Article 7.4 — Width of Exits and Egress Components

The clear width of every egress component shall be calculated by multiplying the occupant load served by 5.0 mm per person for level components such as corridors, doorways and ramps, and by 8.0 mm per person for stairways. In no case shall the clear width be less than 1.10 m for corridors and stairways serving an occupant load exceeding fifty (50) persons, 0.90 m for corridors serving fifty persons or fewer, and 0.80 m for any egress doorway. Handrails may project into the required stairway width by not more than 0.09 m on each side, and door leaves in any position of swing shall not reduce the required egress width by more than one-half.

### Article 7.5 — Exit Doors and Hardware

Every door serving as an exit from a space with an occupant load exceeding fifty (50) persons shall swing in the direction of egress travel. Exit doors shall be openable from the egress side without the use of a key, tool, special knowledge or effort exceeding 65 N of applied force. Where security locking is necessary, panic hardware or fire exit hardware shall be installed. Revolving doors, sliding doors, folding doors and turnstiles shall not be credited as required exits, except that a horizontally sliding door may be credited where it is power-operated, fails safe in the open position on loss of power, and is provided with a clearly marked manual override.

### Article 7.6 — Stairway Construction and Dimensions

Every stairway forming part of a means of egress shall have a uniform riser height of not less than 0.15 m and not more than 0.18 m, and a tread depth of not less than 0.28 m measured between the nosings of consecutive treads. The variation in riser height or tread depth within any single flight shall not exceed 0.005 m. No single flight shall contain more than sixteen (16) risers or fewer than three (3) risers. Landings shall be provided at the top and bottom of every flight, shall have a length in the direction of travel not less than the width of the stairway, and shall have a maximum length of 3.0 m where the stairway changes direction.

### Article 7.7 — Handrails on Stairways and Ramps

Handrails shall be provided on both sides of every stairway and ramp forming part of a means of egress. The upper surface of the handrail shall be located at a height of not less than 0.86 m and not more than 0.96 m measured vertically from the nosing of the tread or from the ramp surface. Handrails shall have a circular cross-section of 32 mm to 50 mm in diameter, or an equivalent graspable profile with a perimeter of 100 mm to 160 mm and a maximum cross-sectional dimension of 57 mm. A clear space of not less than 40 mm shall be maintained between the handrail and any adjacent wall surface, and handrails shall extend horizontally not less than 0.30 m beyond the top and bottom risers.

### Article 7.8 — Ramps Within Means of Egress

A ramp forming part of a means of egress shall have a running slope not exceeding one in twelve (1:12) and a cross slope not exceeding one in fifty (1:50). The maximum rise of any single ramp run shall be 0.75 m, after which a level landing not less than 1.50 m in length shall be provided. Landings shall be provided at the top and bottom of every ramp and at every change of direction, and a landing at a change of direction shall be not less than 1.50 m by 1.50 m. Ramp surfaces shall be slip-resistant under both dry and wet conditions, and edge protection of not less than 0.10 m in height shall be provided along any open side of a ramp.

### Article 7.9 — Exit Signage and Wayfinding

Illuminated exit signs shall be installed at every exit door, at every change of direction within an escape route, and at intervals along corridors such that no point in the escape route is more than 25.0 m from a visible exit sign. Exit signs shall display a legible pictogram with a minimum letter or symbol height of 100 mm, shall be illuminated to a luminance of not less than 2 cd/m² on the safety colour, and shall be connected to the emergency power supply required by Article 7.10 of this Code. Directional arrows shall be provided where the direction of travel to the nearest exit is not immediately apparent.

### Article 7.10 — Emergency Lighting of Escape Routes

Emergency lighting shall be provided throughout all escape routes, protected stairways, lift lobbies, plant rooms, and any space with an occupant load exceeding fifty (50) persons. Emergency lighting shall activate automatically upon failure of the normal supply within five (5) seconds and shall maintain a horizontal illuminance of not less than 1 lx along the centreline of the escape route and not less than 5 lx at stair treads, changes of level, exit doors, fire alarm call points and fire-fighting equipment locations. The emergency lighting supply shall sustain the required illuminance for a minimum duration of ninety (90) minutes and shall be tested monthly by a short-duration functional test and annually by a full-duration discharge test.

### Article 7.11 — Assembly Points and Exit Discharge

Every exit shall discharge directly to a public way, or to a court, yard or open space having a direct, unobstructed path of not less than 1.20 m in width leading to a public way. An external assembly point shall be designated for every building, located at a distance from the building not less than one-half of the building height and providing not less than 0.30 m² of standing area per occupant. The path from the exit discharge to the assembly point shall be free of parked vehicles, refuse storage and landscaping obstructions, and shall be illuminated to not less than 10 lx during hours of darkness.

## Chapter 8 — Accessibility for Persons with Disabilities

### Article 8.1 — Accessible Route to and Within the Building

At least one accessible route shall be provided from the site boundary, from accessible parking spaces, and from any passenger drop-off point to the principal entrance of every building subject to this Code. The accessible route shall have a minimum clear width of 1.20 m, shall be free of steps, shall have a running slope not exceeding one in twenty (1:20) except where designed as a ramp complying with Article 7.8 of this Code, and shall have a firm, stable, slip-resistant surface. Where the accessible route is less than 1.50 m in width, passing places of not less than 1.80 m by 1.80 m shall be provided at intervals not exceeding 25.0 m.

### Article 8.2 — Accessible Entrances and Doors

Not less than sixty percent (60%) of the public entrances of a building, and in all cases the principal entrance, shall be accessible. An accessible door shall provide a clear opening width of not less than 0.85 m when the door leaf is open at ninety degrees (90°). A level manoeuvring space of not less than 1.50 m by 1.50 m shall be provided on both sides of an accessible door. The force required to open a non-fire-rated interior door shall not exceed 22 N, and thresholds shall not exceed 0.015 m in height, or 0.020 m where bevelled at a slope not steeper than one in two (1:2). Door hardware shall be operable with a closed fist and without tight grasping, pinching or twisting of the wrist.

### Article 8.3 — Accessible Sanitary Facilities

Where sanitary facilities are provided for public or employee use, at least one accessible water closet compartment shall be provided on each storey served by an accessible route. An accessible water closet compartment shall have minimum internal dimensions of 1.70 m by 2.20 m, shall contain a clear turning circle of not less than 1.50 m in diameter, and shall provide a lateral transfer space of not less than 0.90 m beside the water closet. The water closet seat shall be located between 0.45 m and 0.48 m above the finished floor. Grab bars of 32 mm to 40 mm diameter shall be installed horizontally at a height of 0.75 m on the wall adjacent to the water closet and as a drop-down rail on the transfer side, each designed to resist a load of not less than 1.5 kN applied in any direction.

### Article 8.4 — Accessible Parking Provision

Accessible parking spaces shall be provided at the following minimum rates: one space where the total parking provision is between one and twenty-five spaces; two spaces for twenty-six to fifty spaces; three spaces for fifty-one to seventy-five spaces; and thereafter one additional accessible space for every fifty spaces or part thereof. An accessible parking space shall measure not less than 3.60 m in width by 5.00 m in length, or 2.50 m in width with an adjacent access aisle of not less than 1.20 m in width. Accessible parking spaces shall be located on the shortest accessible route to an accessible entrance, at a travel distance not exceeding 50.0 m, and shall be marked with the international symbol of accessibility both on the pavement and on a vertical sign.

### Article 8.5 — Lifts and Vertical Accessibility

Every building exceeding two (2) storeys and every building providing public services above or below the level of the principal entrance shall be provided with at least one passenger lift serving all occupied storeys. An accessible passenger lift shall have a car of minimum internal dimensions 1.10 m in width by 1.40 m in depth, a clear door opening width of not less than 0.80 m, and a levelling accuracy at each landing of not more than 0.010 m. Control buttons shall be located between 0.90 m and 1.20 m above the finished floor, shall incorporate raised tactile characters and Braille, and shall be accompanied by audible floor announcement and visual floor indication within the car.

### Article 8.6 — Tactile and Visual Wayfinding

Tactile warning surfaces of a corduroy or blister profile shall be installed at the head and foot of every external stairway, at the approach to every pedestrian crossing on the accessible route, and at the edge of every unprotected drop exceeding 0.15 m. Directional signage on accessible routes shall use a sans-serif typeface with a character height of not less than 25 mm for signs viewed from 2.0 m, increasing proportionally with viewing distance, and shall provide a luminance contrast of not less than thirty percent (30%) between the characters and their background. Glazed doors and full-height glazed screens shall be marked with two horizontal contrasting bands located at heights of 0.85 m to 1.00 m and 1.40 m to 1.60 m above the finished floor.

### Article 8.7 — Accessible Dwelling Units in Multi-Unit Residential Buildings

In residential buildings containing more than twenty (20) dwelling units, not less than five percent (5%) of the dwelling units, and in no case fewer than one unit, shall be constructed as fully accessible units. A fully accessible dwelling unit shall provide an accessible route throughout the unit with a minimum clear corridor width of 1.20 m, door clear opening widths of not less than 0.85 m, a turning circle of 1.50 m diameter in the living room, the principal bedroom and the accessible bathroom, kitchen work surfaces with a clear knee space not less than 0.70 m in height, and electrical switches and outlets located between 0.45 m and 1.20 m above the finished floor.

### Article 8.8 — Assistive Listening and Emergency Notification

Assembly spaces with an occupant load exceeding fifty (50) persons and all spaces in which audible communication is integral to the function of the space, including lecture halls, courtrooms, places of worship and ticket counters, shall be provided with an assistive listening system covering not less than fifty percent (50%) of the seating area. Buildings equipped with a fire alarm system in accordance with Article 6.8 of this Code shall additionally provide visual alarm notification appliances in all accessible sanitary facilities, in all accessible dwelling units, in corridors, and in every space where the ambient sound level regularly exceeds 75 dB(A).

## Chapter 9 — Electrical Systems

### Article 9.1 — General Requirements for Electrical Installations

All electrical installations shall be designed, installed, inspected and tested by a licensed electrical contractor under the supervision of a licensed electrical engineer. Electrical equipment and materials shall be certified by an accredited certification body as complying with a recognized product standard. Every electrical installation shall be provided with a main isolating device located in an accessible position at the point of supply, and shall be documented by a set of as-built drawings, a schedule of circuits and a signed test certificate recording insulation resistance, earth continuity, earth fault loop impedance and residual current device operating times.

### Article 9.2 — Earthing and Bonding

Every building shall be provided with an earthing system comprising an earth electrode or electrode array achieving a measured earth resistance not exceeding 5 ohms for general installations and not exceeding 1 ohm where sensitive electronic equipment or medical equipment is installed. A main earthing terminal shall be installed adjacent to the main distribution board, and main protective bonding conductors shall connect the main earthing terminal to incoming metallic water pipes, gas pipes, structural steel and metallic ventilation ducting. Supplementary bonding shall be installed in bathrooms, shower rooms and swimming pool enclosures connecting all simultaneously accessible exposed and extraneous conductive parts.

### Article 9.3 — Residual Current Protection

Residual current devices with a rated residual operating current not exceeding 30 mA and an operating time not exceeding 40 milliseconds at five times the rated residual current shall protect all of the following: socket outlet circuits rated at 32 A or less intended for general use; circuits supplying equipment in bathrooms, kitchens, laundry rooms and external locations; circuits supplying portable equipment used outdoors; and all lighting circuits within dwelling units. Residual current devices shall be fitted with a manual test facility, and the occupant shall be instructed in writing to operate the test facility at intervals not exceeding six (6) months.

### Article 9.4 — Socket Outlets and Switch Locations

Socket outlets shall be installed at a height of not less than 0.30 m above the finished floor in general locations and not less than 0.15 m above any adjacent work surface in kitchens. Switches for lighting shall be installed at a height of between 0.90 m and 1.20 m above the finished floor. In dwelling units, the minimum provision of socket outlets shall be six (6) double outlets in the principal living room, four (4) double outlets in each bedroom, six (6) double outlets in the kitchen of which not fewer than four are located above the work surface, and one (1) double outlet in each corridor of a length exceeding 3.0 m. Socket outlets shall not be installed within 0.60 m horizontally of a bathtub or shower enclosure.

### Article 9.5 — Wiring Methods and Cable Installation

Cables concealed within walls shall be installed within prescribed zones located within 0.15 m of the top of the wall, within 0.15 m of an internal corner, or in a vertical or horizontal line directly above, below or to the side of an accessory. Cables installed outside these zones at a depth of less than 0.05 m from the finished surface shall be enclosed in earthed metallic conduit or mechanically protected by a steel plate. Cables shall not be installed in contact with thermal insulation without derating in accordance with the manufacturer's data. Cable penetrations of fire-rated construction shall be sealed in accordance with Article 6.2 of this Code.

### Article 9.6 — Distribution Boards and Electrical Rooms

Distribution boards shall be installed in accessible locations with a clear working space of not less than 0.80 m in depth and 1.00 m in width in front of the board, and shall not be installed within bathrooms, above cooking appliances, within stairway enclosures forming part of a protected escape route, or in any location where they obstruct a means of egress. Every circuit shall be permanently and legibly identified on a schedule affixed to the interior of the board. Electrical rooms containing transformers or switchgear rated above 100 kVA shall be enclosed in construction having a fire-resistance rating of not less than 120 minutes and shall be provided with ventilation sufficient to limit the internal temperature rise to 15 °C above ambient.

### Article 9.7 — Secondary and Emergency Power Supply

A secondary power supply shall be provided for the following loads in buildings exceeding six (6) storeys, in institutional occupancies, and in assembly occupancies with an occupant load exceeding three hundred (300) persons: emergency lighting, exit signage, fire alarm and detection systems, fire pumps, smoke control fans, fire-fighting lifts, and security systems controlling egress doors. The secondary supply shall be provided by a generator set or a central battery system capable of assuming the connected load within fifteen (15) seconds of loss of the normal supply and sustaining it for a minimum duration of three (3) hours. Fuel storage for generator sets shall comply with the flammable material storage requirements of Article 6.12 of this Code.

### Article 9.8 — Lightning Protection

A lightning protection system shall be installed on every building exceeding 20.0 m in height, every building housing an assembly or institutional occupancy irrespective of height, every structure containing flammable or explosive materials, and every building situated on an exposed elevated site. The system shall comprise an air termination network, down conductors spaced at intervals not exceeding 20.0 m around the building perimeter, and an earth termination network bonded to the main earthing terminal required by Article 9.2 of this Code. The continuity and earth resistance of the lightning protection system shall be tested at intervals not exceeding twenty-four (24) months and recorded in the building log book.

## Chapter 10 — Plumbing, Drainage and Sanitary Systems

### Article 10.1 — Potable Water Supply and Quality Protection

Every building intended for human occupancy shall be connected to a potable water supply of sufficient pressure and flow to serve all installed fixtures simultaneously at their design flow rates. The water service shall be provided with an isolating valve accessible from outside the building and a backflow prevention device appropriate to the degree of hazard presented by the installation. Cross-connection between a potable water system and any non-potable system, including irrigation, fire suppression and greywater systems, is prohibited. Materials in contact with potable water shall be certified as non-toxic and shall not impart taste, odour or colour to the water.

### Article 10.2 — Minimum Water Supply Pressures and Fixture Flow Rates

The static water pressure at any fixture shall be not less than 100 kPa and not more than 500 kPa. Where the supply pressure exceeds 500 kPa, a pressure-reducing valve shall be installed. Minimum design flow rates shall be 0.10 litres per second for a washbasin, 0.15 litres per second for a kitchen sink, 0.20 litres per second for a shower, 0.30 litres per second for a bathtub and 0.10 litres per second for a water closet cistern. Water storage tanks, where installed, shall have a capacity of not less than 500 litres per dwelling unit, shall be constructed of certified non-toxic materials, shall be fitted with a close-fitting lid and an insect-proof vent, and shall be accessible for cleaning and inspection.

### Article 10.3 — Hot Water Systems and Scald Protection

Hot water for personal washing shall be delivered at a temperature not exceeding 48 °C at the point of use in residential and institutional occupancies, achieved by means of a thermostatic mixing valve installed as close as practicable to the outlet. Water within a storage water heater shall be maintained at a temperature of not less than 60 °C to inhibit bacterial growth, and the system shall be capable of raising the entire stored volume to 60 °C at least once in every twenty-four (24) hour period. Every storage water heater shall be fitted with a temperature and pressure relief valve discharging to a visible, safe location through a pipe of not less than the nominal size of the valve outlet and without any intervening valve.

### Article 10.4 — Minimum Sanitary Fixture Provision

Sanitary fixtures shall be provided at the following minimum rates. In dwelling units: one water closet, one washbasin, and one bathtub or shower per unit. In office occupancies: one water closet per twenty-five (25) persons of each sex up to fifty persons and one additional per fifty persons thereafter, with one washbasin per two water closets. In educational occupancies: one water closet per thirty (30) pupils and one washbasin per forty (40) pupils. In assembly occupancies: one water closet per one hundred (100) male occupants and per fifty (50) female occupants, with one washbasin per two hundred (200) occupants. Accessible sanitary facilities required by Article 8.3 of this Code may be counted toward these minimum provisions.

### Article 10.5 — Drainage System Design and Gradients

Sanitary drainage pipework shall be sized and laid to convey the design discharge without surcharge, with a minimum gradient of one in forty (1:40) for pipes of 100 mm nominal diameter, one in sixty (1:60) for pipes of 150 mm nominal diameter and one in one hundred (1:100) for pipes exceeding 150 mm nominal diameter. The minimum nominal diameter shall be 40 mm for a washbasin branch, 50 mm for a sink, shower or bathtub branch, and 100 mm for a water closet branch and for any drain serving a water closet. Changes of direction shall be made with long-radius fittings, and rodding access shall be provided at every change of direction exceeding forty-five degrees (45°), at every junction, and at intervals not exceeding 20.0 m on straight runs.

### Article 10.6 — Traps, Water Seals and Venting

Every sanitary fixture shall be provided with a trap maintaining a water seal of not less than 50 mm for fixtures connected to a ventilated branch, and not less than 75 mm for fixtures discharging to a drain that also serves a water closet or to an unventilated branch. Drainage systems shall be vented to the atmosphere so that the pressure fluctuation within the system does not exceed plus or minus 375 Pa under design discharge conditions, thereby preventing the loss of trap seals. Vent pipes shall terminate not less than 0.90 m above any openable window, air intake or ventilation opening located within a horizontal distance of 3.0 m. Interconnection of a drainage vent with a mechanical ventilation duct or a flue is prohibited.

### Article 10.7 — Rainwater Drainage and Roof Discharge

Roof drainage systems shall be designed for a rainfall intensity of not less than 75 mm per hour over a five-minute duration with a return period of not less than twenty (20) years. Every roof shall be laid to a minimum fall of one in eighty (1:80) toward outlets or gutters. Where an internal rainwater drainage system is used, a secondary overflow system of equal capacity shall be provided, discharging to a visible location at the building exterior to give notice of blockage of the primary system. Rainwater downpipes shall not discharge into the sanitary drainage system, and rainwater shall not be permitted to discharge onto an adjoining property or onto a public footway.

### Article 10.8 — Greywater, Wastewater and Subsoil Drainage

Where a greywater recycling system is installed, all greywater pipework and outlets shall be permanently marked in a distinguishing colour and labelled as non-potable, and all greywater outlets accessible to occupants shall be fitted with a removable key or locking device. Buildings not connected to a public sewer shall be served by an approved on-site wastewater treatment system located not less than 15.0 m from any water well, not less than 5.0 m from any building foundation and not less than 2.0 m from any site boundary. Subsoil drainage shall be installed around basement perimeters, discharging to a positive outfall or to a sump fitted with duty and standby pumps each capable of handling the full design inflow.

### Article 10.9 — Pipework Protection, Insulation and Testing

Buried pipework shall be laid on a granular bed of not less than 0.10 m thickness and shall be provided with a minimum cover of 0.60 m beneath areas subject to vehicular loading and 0.30 m elsewhere, or shall be protected by a concrete surround. Pipework passing through structural elements shall be sleeved to permit movement without transferring structural load to the pipe. Hot water distribution pipework shall be thermally insulated to limit heat loss, and cold water pipework located in unheated spaces or externally shall be insulated to prevent condensation. All drainage systems shall be subjected to an air or water pressure test prior to concealment and shall sustain the test pressure for not less than three (3) minutes without loss.

## Chapter 11 — Ventilation, Thermal Performance and Acoustics

### Article 11.1 — Natural Ventilation of Habitable Rooms

Every habitable room shall be provided with natural ventilation by means of openable windows, doors or ventilators communicating directly with the external air. The aggregate free openable area shall be not less than five percent (5%) of the net floor area of the room served. Where a room is ventilated by openings in a single wall, the depth of the room measured perpendicular to that wall shall not exceed 2.5 times the clear ceiling height of the room. Where cross-ventilation is provided by openings in two opposing or adjacent walls, the permitted room depth may be increased to 5.0 times the clear ceiling height.

### Article 11.2 — Mechanical Ventilation Rates

Where natural ventilation complying with Article 11.1 of this Code cannot be achieved, mechanical ventilation shall be provided at the following minimum extract rates: bathrooms and shower rooms, 15 litres per second; water closet compartments, 6 litres per second; kitchens, 30 litres per second where the extract point is located above the cooking appliance and 60 litres per second elsewhere; and utility and laundry rooms, 15 litres per second. Minimum outdoor air supply rates shall be 8 litres per second per person in offices and classrooms, 10 litres per second per person in assembly spaces, and 0.35 air changes per hour continuously in dwelling units. Extract air from sanitary facilities, kitchens and plant rooms shall not be recirculated to any occupied space.

### Article 11.3 — Ventilation of Car Parks and Plant Rooms

Enclosed car parks shall be provided with ventilation achieving not less than six (6) air changes per hour under normal operation and not less than ten (10) air changes per hour under fire mode operation, with the system arranged to extract from both high and low levels. Carbon monoxide monitoring shall be installed in enclosed car parks exceeding 500 m², configured to increase ventilation when the concentration exceeds 50 parts per million averaged over eight hours and to activate an audible and visual alarm at 200 parts per million. Plant rooms containing fuel-fired equipment shall be provided with permanent combustion air openings of not less than 0.005 m² per kilowatt of rated heat input, distributed between high and low level.

### Article 11.4 — Thermal Insulation of the Building Envelope

The building envelope shall be constructed to limit heat transfer, with maximum thermal transmittance (U-value) values as follows: external walls, 0.50 W/m²·K; roofs, 0.35 W/m²·K; floors in contact with the ground, 0.55 W/m²·K; and glazing including frames, 2.20 W/m²·K. Thermal bridging at junctions, lintels, jambs, sills and balcony connections shall be limited by continuous insulation or by thermal break elements. Where the aggregate glazed area of a façade exceeds forty percent (40%) of the façade area, compensatory measures such as external shading, improved glazing performance or a reduced envelope U-value shall be demonstrated by whole-building energy calculation.

### Article 11.5 — Control of Condensation and Moisture

External wall, roof and floor assemblies shall be designed so that the internal surface temperature under design winter conditions does not fall below the dew point of the internal air, and so that interstitial condensation does not accumulate within the assembly over an annual cycle. A vapour control layer shall be installed on the warm side of the thermal insulation in assemblies enclosing spaces of high humidity, including kitchens, bathrooms, laundries and swimming pool halls. A damp-proof course shall be installed in all external and internal walls at a level not less than 0.15 m above the adjacent finished external ground level, and a damp-proof membrane shall be installed beneath all ground-bearing floor slabs.

### Article 11.6 — Airborne and Impact Sound Insulation

Separating walls and floors between dwelling units, and between a dwelling unit and any common area, plant room, lift shaft or commercial space, shall achieve a weighted standardized level difference for airborne sound of not less than 52 dB. Separating floors shall additionally achieve a weighted standardized impact sound pressure level of not more than 58 dB. Walls separating classrooms from corridors shall achieve an airborne sound insulation value of not less than 45 dB. Plant rooms, lift motor rooms, pump rooms and refuse chutes shall not share a wall or floor with a sleeping room unless isolating construction is provided and the resulting internal noise level in the sleeping room does not exceed 30 dB(A) at night.

### Article 11.7 — Control of Noise from Building Services

Noise generated by building services equipment, including ventilation plant, pumps, generators, chillers and lift machinery, shall not produce a sound pressure level within any habitable room exceeding 35 dB(A) during daytime hours and 30 dB(A) during night-time hours, measured with all windows closed. Externally mounted equipment shall not produce a sound pressure level exceeding 45 dB(A) at the boundary of the nearest residential property. Vibration-generating equipment shall be mounted on resilient anti-vibration mountings selected to achieve not less than ninety percent (90%) isolation efficiency, and all pipework and ductwork connections to such equipment shall incorporate flexible connectors.

## Chapter 12 — Site Works, Maintenance and Operational Compliance

### Article 12.1 — Site Safety During Construction

The permit holder shall enclose every construction site with a continuous hoarding of not less than 2.00 m in height where the site adjoins a public way or an occupied property. Where construction occurs above a public footway, a protective covered walkway with a clear height of not less than 2.40 m and a structural deck designed for an impact load shall be provided. Excavations exceeding 1.20 m in depth shall be shored, battered or benched under the direction of a competent person, and shall be provided with a means of access and egress at intervals not exceeding 7.5 m along the length of the excavation.

### Article 12.2 — Protection of Adjoining Property

Before commencing excavation, demolition or underpinning within a horizontal distance of the adjoining boundary equal to the depth of the proposed excavation, the permit holder shall carry out and record a condition survey of the adjoining structures, shall notify the adjoining owner in writing not less than thirty (30) days in advance, and shall install monitoring instrumentation to record movement of the adjoining structure. Where measured settlement of an adjoining structure exceeds 0.010 m or angular distortion exceeds one in five hundred (1:500), works shall be suspended and a remedial proposal submitted to the Authority before resumption.

### Article 12.3 — Demolition Requirements

Demolition shall be carried out in accordance with a method statement prepared by a licensed engineer, submitted to and approved by the Authority prior to commencement. The method statement shall address the sequence of demolition, the stability of retained structures at every stage, the disconnection and making safe of all utility services, the control of dust and noise, the identification and removal of hazardous materials including asbestos, and the protection of the public. Demolition shall proceed generally in the reverse order of construction, and no structural element shall be removed while it supports any other element not independently propped.

### Article 12.4 — Waste Management and Site Restoration

Construction and demolition waste shall be segregated on site into inert material, recyclable material, general waste and hazardous waste, and shall be transported to a licensed receiving facility by a licensed carrier. The permit holder shall maintain waste transfer records for a period of not less than three (3) years and shall produce them to the Authority on request. Upon completion of works, the site shall be cleared of all surplus materials, plant and temporary works, and any damage caused to public infrastructure including footways, kerbs, drainage and street furniture shall be reinstated at the expense of the permit holder.

### Article 12.5 — Periodic Inspection and Maintenance of Fire Safety Systems

The owner or operator of a building shall maintain all fire safety systems in an operational condition and shall procure the following inspections and tests: weekly functional testing of the fire alarm system from a different manual call point each week; monthly visual inspection of portable fire extinguishers, fire doors and escape route obstructions; quarterly inspection and testing of automatic sprinkler system valves, gauges and flow switches; six-monthly servicing of fire detection and alarm systems by a competent person; and annual full-duration testing of emergency lighting, annual servicing of portable fire extinguishers and annual flow testing of fire pumps. Records shall be retained in a building log book maintained on the premises.

### Article 12.6 — Structural Condition Assessment of Existing Buildings

The owner of a building exceeding four (4) storeys in height, and of any building of assembly, institutional or educational occupancy, shall procure a structural condition assessment by a licensed structural engineer at intervals not exceeding ten (10) years from the date of the Certificate of Occupancy, and at intervals not exceeding five (5) years for buildings exceeding thirty (30) years of age. The assessment shall report on visible cracking, deflection, corrosion of reinforcement, spalling of concrete cover, water ingress, the condition of façade fixings and the condition of balconies and cantilevered elements. Defects classified as affecting structural safety shall be reported to the Authority within seven (7) days of identification.

### Article 12.7 — Façade and Cladding Maintenance

The owner of a building with an external façade of stone, tile, glass, metal panel or rendered finish situated above a public way or accessible area shall procure a close visual inspection of the façade fixings and anchorages at intervals not exceeding six (6) years, and following any seismic event of a magnitude sufficient to be felt at the site. Loose, cracked or displaced façade elements shall be secured or removed immediately, and the area beneath shall be cordoned off until the hazard is eliminated. Records of façade inspections, including photographic documentation and the identification of the inspecting engineer, shall be retained in the building log book referred to in Article 12.5 of this Code.

### Article 12.8 — Building Log Book and Operating Documentation

Upon completion of construction and as a condition of the issuance of the Certificate of Occupancy required by Article 1.7 of this Code, the permit holder shall deliver to the owner a building log book containing as-built drawings of the architectural, structural, electrical, mechanical and plumbing installations; test and commissioning certificates for all building services; fire safety system design documentation including the design occupant load and the evacuation strategy; operation and maintenance manuals for all installed equipment; and a schedule of required periodic inspections. The building log book shall be retained on the premises, shall be transferred to any successor owner upon change of ownership, and shall be produced to the Authority upon request.

### Article 12.9 — Change of Use and Re-Evaluation of Compliance

Where the occupancy classification of a building or a portion of a building is changed from one group defined in Article 2.6 of this Code to another, the owner shall submit an application to the Authority accompanied by an assessment demonstrating compliance of the building in its new use with the requirements of this Code relating to occupant load, means of egress, fire-resistance ratings, sanitary fixture provision, accessibility and structural live load capacity. No change of use shall be implemented, and no Certificate of Occupancy for the new classification shall be issued, until the Authority has approved the assessment and any consequential alteration works have been completed and inspected.

### Article 12.10 — Effective Date and Transitional Arrangements

This Code takes effect one hundred eighty (180) days after the date of its publication by the Authority. An application for a building permit that was complete and formally submitted before the effective date may be assessed under the edition of the building regulations in force at the date of submission, provided that construction commences within twelve (12) months of the issuance of the permit. Any application submitted on or after the effective date shall be assessed exclusively under this Code. Where an existing building is the subject of an enforcement action commenced before the effective date, the proceedings shall continue under the regulations in force at the date of commencement of the action.
